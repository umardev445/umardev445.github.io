Course overview

Learn Tajweed online through explanation and live correction

Knowing the name of a Tajweed rule is different from applying it while reading. A learner may understand an example in isolation but return to an old pronunciation habit when several words are joined in a verse. QuranCrest's online Tajweed classes therefore combine short explanations with recitation, listening and immediate correction.

The first assessment checks the learner's current reading, including letter articulation, common vowel patterns, pace and repeated mistakes. The tutor can then select a small number of priorities instead of presenting every rule at once. A reader with weak joining or letter recognition may first be advised to review Noorani Qaida or guided Quran Reading.

Lessons are live and one-to-one for children and adults. The learning path can include makharij, relevant letter qualities, rules that appear in the learner's passage, elongation, echoing sounds and careful stopping. The order and depth depend on the learner's starting level; this outline is a pathway rather than a fixed completion promise.

Online Tajweed Classes curriculum and learning path

The exact starting point and pace depend on the initial assessment. These stages show the skills a learner can work through without promising an identical timeline for every student.

1. Recitation and makharij assessment

Read a suitable passage aloud so the tutor can identify articulation, vowel, joining, nasalization, elongation and stopping patterns that need attention.

2. Letter articulation and sound quality

Practise difficult Arabic letters from their points of articulation and compare commonly confused sounds before applying them inside words and verses.

3. Noon, Tanween and Meem patterns

Study the relevant rules progressively and hear how they change connected recitation, with examples selected from the learner's reading rather than memorized as definitions only.

4. Madd, Qalqalah and controlled timing

Work on elongation and echoing sounds at the level appropriate to the learner, using measured repetition to reduce both overextension and rushed reading.

5. Applying rules in continuous recitation

Read longer passages while maintaining the rules already studied, with focused correction for the mistakes that return when attention is divided.

6. Waqf, restarting and personal review

Develop more careful stopping and restarting habits, then maintain a correction list that connects each lesson with the next practice target.

Who should take this course?

  • Quran readers who want practical Tajweed correction rather than rules alone
  • Adults returning to recitation after a long break
  • Children who can read connected Quranic text and are ready for focused correction
  • Learners who confuse particular Arabic letters or repeat the same sound patterns
  • Readers preparing for a memorization plan who first need steadier recitation
  • English- or Urdu-speaking learners who prefer private live instruction

How a live one-to-one lesson works

Listen to the current reading

The learner recites a review passage while the tutor identifies which earlier correction is secure and which one still needs attention.

Explain one practical priority

A relevant articulation point or rule is demonstrated with a small number of clear examples connected to the learner's passage.

Apply, correct and repeat

The learner reads the target words and verses aloud, receives immediate feedback and repeats the corrected section without rushing.

Record the practice focus

The lesson ends with a specific passage and one or two correction priorities to revisit before the next live class.

Support shaped around the learner

Tajweed classes for developing readers

A learner who is still guessing letters or struggling to join words may progress better by strengthening the reading foundation first. The assessment helps decide whether to begin with Qaida, Quran Reading or focused Tajweed.

Tajweed for kids and adults

Children may use shorter examples and more repetition, while adults may prefer clearer rule explanations and notes about long-standing habits. Both still need to recite aloud for the tutor to hear the actual application.

How to practise between lessons

  • Review the exact words and verses corrected in class before moving to a new passage.
  • Practise one articulation or rule priority at a time instead of trying to monitor every rule together.
  • Read aloud at a controlled pace so difficult sounds and timings can be heard clearly.
  • Keep a short personal correction list and remove an item only when it is applied consistently.
  • Use a reliable recitation selected with the tutor for listening practice, but do not replace live correction with imitation alone.

How progress and readiness are reviewed

Finishing pages alone does not confirm progress. The tutor can recommend the next stage when the learner:

  • distinguishes previously confused letters more consistently
  • applies the studied rule in a new example with less prompting
  • maintains accuracy when moving from isolated words to a longer passage
  • stops and restarts with greater care at the level already taught
  • recognizes a recurring mistake and can attempt the correction independently
