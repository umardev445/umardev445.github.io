Course overview

Learn Noorani Qaida online from the first letter

Noorani Qaida is a structured starting point for learners who cannot yet read Arabic confidently. It introduces Arabic letters and their sounds before moving gradually into joined forms, vowel marks and simple Quranic words. Instead of asking a beginner to guess or memorize a page, the tutor demonstrates each sound, listens to the learner and corrects mistakes as they appear.

QuranCrest teaches this foundation through live one-to-one lessons. The starting lesson is selected after a free assessment, so a complete beginner can begin with individual letters while a returning learner can restart from the first skill that needs correction. The aim is not to rush through the book; it is to develop reading habits that prepare the learner for guided Quran reading.

Noorani Qaida for Beginners curriculum and learning path

The exact starting point and pace depend on the initial assessment. These stages show the skills a learner can work through without promising an identical timeline for every student.

1. Arabic letters and clear sounds

Recognize individual Arabic letters and practise their basic sounds with direct tutor demonstration and correction.

2. Letter shapes and joining

Identify how letters change at the beginning, middle and end of words, then read simple joined combinations.

3. Short vowels and Tanween

Read letters and short combinations with Fathah, Kasrah, Dammah and double-vowel patterns without relying on guesswork.

4. Sukoon and Shaddah

Practise resting letters and doubled sounds carefully, with repetition focused on the learner's recurring mistakes.

5. Madd and Leen foundations

Develop an introductory understanding of long and soft sounds so words are read with greater control and consistency.

6. Connected words and Quran-reading preparation

Combine the earlier skills in longer words and selected reading exercises before moving into guided reading from the Quran.

Who should take this course?

  • Children beginning Arabic reading for the first time
  • Adults who want to learn Quran reading from the alphabet
  • Learners returning after a long break or an incomplete foundation
  • Non-Arabic speakers who need patient, step-by-step guidance
  • Learners who recognize some letters but struggle to join or pronounce them

How a live one-to-one lesson works

Review

The learner reads the previous exercise so the tutor can check recognition, pronunciation and retention.

Demonstration

The tutor introduces a small new skill and demonstrates the relevant letter, sign or reading pattern.

Guided reading

The learner reads aloud while the tutor gives immediate, focused correction and asks for careful repetition.

Practice target

The lesson ends with a manageable section to revise before the next class, based on age and current ability.

Support shaped around the learner

Noorani Qaida for kids

Children usually benefit from short explanations, frequent repetition and one clear practice target. Parents can support younger learners by preparing the device, keeping the learning space calm and checking the assigned lines between lessons.

Noorani Qaida for adults

Adult beginners can start from individual letters without embarrassment. Lessons can spend more time on difficult sounds, familiar mistakes and a practice routine that fits work and family responsibilities.

How to practise between lessons

  • Review the exact letters or lines assigned by the tutor instead of moving ahead alone.
  • Practise briefly and regularly, focusing first on accuracy rather than speed.
  • Read aloud so sounds can be heard clearly; silent recognition alone does not build recitation skill.
  • Keep a simple note of letters or combinations that require extra correction.

How progress and readiness are reviewed

Finishing pages alone does not confirm progress. The tutor can recommend the next stage when the learner:

  • recognizes letters consistently in different positions
  • reads common vowel and joining patterns with less prompting
  • applies the pronunciation points already taught with reasonable consistency
  • can approach connected Quranic words without guessing each letter
