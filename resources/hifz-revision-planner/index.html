Quick answer

A balanced Hifz routine protects new memorization with recent and older revision. Use this tool to divide available time between Sabaq, Sabaqi and Manzil, then ask the teacher to set the actual lines or pages after listening to the learner.

The result stays in your browser. It allocates time and does not promise a memorization speed.

What Sabaq, Sabaqi and Manzil mean in this planner

Terminology can differ between teachers. In this tool, Sabaq means the new portion currently being memorized, Sabaqi means recently memorized portions that still need close repetition, and Manzil means older memorization reviewed on a continuing cycle.

The teacher's own terms and method remain final. The important principle is that new memorization should not push recent and older revision out of the schedule.

Why the actual amount must come from a teacher

Two learners with forty-five minutes may need very different portions. Reading accuracy, age, school load, recall, passage difficulty and strength of previous memorization all matter. A time planner can create space; it cannot hear whether yesterday's portion is stable.

Set new memorization after revision quality is known

Before increasing Sabaq, check whether the learner can recite recent lessons without repeated prompting and whether older portions remain available. If errors collect faster than they are repaired, the teacher may reduce new material temporarily. This protects long-term retention and should not be treated as failure.

A practical weekly rhythm

  • Begin each session with the weakest item identified by the teacher.
  • Use the same Mushaf consistently unless the teacher recommends otherwise.
  • Recite aloud so errors can be heard and corrected.
  • Mark repeated errors, then revisit them before adding more.
  • Keep one weekly session focused on repair and review rather than new lines.
  • Report school exams, illness or schedule changes before targets become unrealistic.

How to prepare a new portion

  1. Listen to or read with the teacher's approved model.
  2. Understand the boundaries of the assigned portion.
  3. Read accurately while looking before relying on memory.
  4. Memorize in small connected units.
  5. Join the units and recite without looking.
  6. Return later in the day to test recall after a gap.

Do not increase the portion only because the first repetition felt easy. Delayed recall and connection to the previous passage matter.

How to review older memorization

Use a defined cycle so familiar surahs do not receive all the attention while weaker sections remain avoided. Record the portion, date and recurring errors. The teacher can then decide whether to repeat the same Manzil, shorten it for accuracy or move forward.

Parent support for a child doing Hifz

Parents can protect the study time, listen when qualified to do so, maintain the tracker and communicate workload changes. Avoid comparing daily portions with another child. A smaller stable portion can represent better progress than a larger portion that disappears from revision.

Adult Hifz planning

Adults should account for work, sleep and family responsibilities when selecting the number of study days. A reliable early or late routine may be more useful than waiting for a long free block. Share travel or shift changes so the teacher can adjust new and revision targets.

Frequently asked questions

How many lines should I memorize each day?

There is no universal number. The teacher should decide after checking reading accuracy, recall, available time and revision strength.

Should I stop new memorization when Manzil becomes weak?

The teacher may reduce or pause new material while older revision is repaired. The decision should follow a live review rather than a fixed rule from this tool.

Can I use this planner without a teacher?

It can organize time, but accurate recitation and memorization benefit from someone qualified listening and correcting.

Does QuranCrest save the plan?

No. The calculation happens in the browser. Print or write down the result if you want to keep it.
