How to Perform Ghusl: Hanafi Obligations & Shower Checklist

Understand the three Hanafi obligations of ghusl, a complete washing routine, shower coverage checks, and how ghusl differs from wudu.

Home / Library
By QuranCrest Editorial TeamUpdated 28 October 2026

Quick answer

In Hanafi fiqh, the obligatory washing of ghusl includes the entire mouth, the soft part inside the nose, and the whole external body. A shower can fulfil ghusl when these areas are washed properly. Washing the body while omitting the mouth and nose does not meet this Hanafi minimum.

Fiqh details follow the linked Hanafi sources; other schools may differ. This is source-based educational guidance, not a personal fatwa. Ask a qualified scholar about individual circumstances.

When this guide applies

Ghusl is a ritual bath, not simply a name for every ordinary shower. A major ritual state following intercourse or sexual discharge, and the ending of menstruation or postnatal bleeding, are common reasons an obligatory ghusl is discussed. Unusual discharge, uncertain bleeding patterns and illness need an individual answer rather than a diagnosis from this page.

Have privacy, clean water and a safe surface. Remove an actual barrier to washing. The intention belongs in the heart; you do not have to say a formula aloud in the bathroom.

The three obligatory washing areas

  1. Rinse the whole mouth so water reaches its washable interior.
  2. Wash the soft interior of the nose without forcing water deeply or causing harm.
  3. Wash the entire external body, including the scalp and accessible folds. Water must reach the skin rather than merely the surface of a waterproof coating.

These are the Hanafi obligations. Other schools classify some details differently, so identify the school of the answer you are using. A teacher can show the practical coverage without asking you to disclose private details publicly.

A fuller routine under a shower

  1. Begin with the intention to purify yourself.
  2. Wash the hands and clean any actual impurity from the body.
  3. Perform wudu, including the mouth and nose.
  4. Wash the head and ensure the scalp is reached.
  5. Wash the body thoroughly, attending to the right and left sides, folds and feet.

The fuller sunna bath includes repeated complete washing. A single complete washing of every obligatory area fulfils the minimum. A timer or a large amount of water is not what establishes completion.

Coverage checks without repeated inspection

FeetInclude soles, heels and between toes

Finish after reasonable normal washing. A feeling that something might have been missed is different from knowing a dry area remains. For wounds, dressings or water restrictions, obtain a tailored answer.

Common questions

Can an ordinary shower count as ghusl?

Yes, when the required washing is actually completed. In this Hanafi guide that includes the mouth, nose and entire external body; the shower’s name does not determine validity.

Do I need another wudu after ghusl?

A valid Hanafi ghusl also lifts minor ritual impurity, provided a wudu-breaking event has not occurred afterwards.

Is shampoo required?

Shampoo is not one of the ritual obligations. The issue is water reaching the required areas, including the scalp, with barriers removed.
