Daily Duas for Kids: A Small, Sourced Practice Set

Learn three daily duas with Arabic, simple meanings, hadith references and a printable practice tracker for parents and children.

Start with a small routine

Choose one everyday occasion, help the child hear the words correctly, explain their meaning and practise without turning the moment into a test. This small set covers beginning a meal, going to sleep and waking. References are linked so parents can check the wording in context.

1. At the beginning of a meal

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ

Reading support: Bismillah.

Meaning: Begin in Allah’s name.

Sahih al-Bukhari 5376 records guidance given to a boy about naming Allah at the start of eating, using the right hand and eating from what is near him. A parent can model the short phrase before the meal and gently remind the child when needed.

2. When going to sleep

بِاسْمِكَ اللَّهُمَّ أَمُوتُ وَأَحْيَا

Reading support: Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya.

Meaning in simple words: Address Allah in His name, acknowledging that death and life are under His care.

This wording is reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 6324. Read the full report for context. The simple meaning here is an explanatory paraphrase, not a word-for-word translation.

3. After waking

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَحْيَانَا بَعْدَ مَا أَمَاتَنَا وَإِلَيْهِ النُّشُورُ

Meaning in simple words: Thank Allah for restoring life after sleep and remember that people will return to Him in resurrection.

The waking remembrance is also reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 6324. Let a teacher model the longer Arabic phrase in small parts. Use the referenced narration’s reading support if needed, then move toward listening and accurate Arabic rather than relying only on English letters.

Teach meaning and occasion together

Ask “When do we say this?” before asking the child to repeat it. If the child remembers an occasion but misses a word, model the phrase again calmly. If the child can recite but does not understand the occasion, use a real routine rather than add another memorization target.

Do not expect a child to learn all three at once. A parent might begin with the meal phrase, keep bedtime practice brief and add the waking remembrance when the first task is comfortable. This is an illustrative learning order, not a prescribed religious timetable.

Check pronunciation gently

A written page cannot hear the child’s sounds. Ask the teacher to listen, particularly where English transliteration is being used. Keep the focus on one correction and avoid attaching a negative label to the child when words are still being learned. Families can discuss age-appropriate dua learning through the existing Islamic Studies course.

Frequently asked questions

Are these the only daily duas?

No. This is a deliberately small sourced selection, not a complete collection of daily remembrances.

Are the meanings literal translations?

The meanings are brief explanatory paraphrases. Consult the linked narrations for their full text and translation.

Can a parent print the tracker?

Yes. Use the print button and record practice on paper. The page does not submit the child’s practice information.
