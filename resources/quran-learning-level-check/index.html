Quick answer

A useful starting level depends on what the learner can recognize, join, read aloud and correct. Complete this short self-check, then use a live assessment to confirm the course. No answer is transmitted or stored by QuranCrest.

Check the learner's likely starting level

Choose the answer that describes the learner today. Do not choose the level you hope to reach. An honest starting point prevents both unnecessary repetition and lessons that move too quickly.

1. Can the learner recognize most Arabic letters in their different shapes?
2. Can the learner join letters and read short vowel patterns?
3. Can the learner read a short passage directly from the Mushaf?
4. What is the main learning goal?

Five practical Quran learning stages

Course names vary between academies, so the skill behind the name matters more than the label. The following stages describe observable abilities a tutor can listen for.

StageWhat the learner can doLikely next focus
Letter foundationRecognizes some letters but confuses shapes, dots or sounds.Arabic letters, clear sound models and careful recognition.
Qaida developmentKnows letters but needs help with vowels, joining, sukoon or shaddah patterns.Short patterns, joined words and repeated accurate reading.
Guided Mushaf readingCan enter the Mushaf but reads slowly, loses the place or repeats common errors.Word tracking, pronunciation, stopping and steady fluency.
Tajweed applicationReads independently but needs systematic correction of makharij and relevant rules.Apply one rule at a time during real recitation.

Why age alone cannot determine a level

A ten-year-old may read confidently while an adult is beginning with letters. Another adult may have memorized several surahs but repeat pronunciation errors learned years earlier. Placement should therefore start with a short reading sample, not an assumption based on age, previous course certificates or pages completed.

For children, a tutor should also notice attention span, confidence and whether the learner can follow a short instruction. For adults, the assessment should respect existing knowledge and avoid sending everyone back to the first Qaida page.

What a live assessment should check

  • Letter recognition and common sound confusion
  • Vowels, joining and tracking across a line
  • Ability to read unfamiliar words rather than only memorized passages
  • Repeated pronunciation or stopping patterns
  • Current memorization and strength of older revision
  • Language, time zone, tutor preference and realistic weekly schedule

A useful assessment does not need to embarrass or test the learner under pressure. Its purpose is to find the first lesson that will produce progress.

How to prepare a child for the assessment

Tell the child that the tutor is listening to understand where to begin, not to give a pass or fail result. Keep the current Qaida or Mushaf nearby. A parent can remain present for a younger child while allowing the tutor to hear the learner's own reading.

How adults can get an accurate recommendation

Share honestly whether you recognize letters, read only familiar surahs or can read a new page independently. Mention a long break, a previous teacher or a particular pronunciation concern. Private one-to-one assessment makes it possible to restart without comparison with a group.

Frequently asked questions

Is this checker a formal placement test?

No. It suggests a likely starting point. A tutor must listen before confirming the course.

Should every beginner complete the full Noorani Qaida?

No. A learner may need the full foundation, selected revision or direct guided reading. The assessment should decide.

Can a learner start Hifz with weak reading?

Reading and pronunciation readiness should be checked first. The tutor may recommend correcting the foundation before increasing memorization.

Does QuranCrest receive these answers?

No. The result is calculated in the browser. Information is sent only if you separately submit the assessment form.
