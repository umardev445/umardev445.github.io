What determines the rule?

Find noon sakinah or tanween, then look at the following letter while continuing recitation. That letter helps identify Izhar, Idgham, Iqlab or Ikhfa. Naming the rule is the recognition task; learning its sound requires a correct model and feedback.

Recognize the starting sign

Noon sakinah is a noon without a vowel. Tanween marks indicate an added n-sound at a noun’s end when continuing. Do not apply a connected-reading example automatically when stopping. The About Tajweed introduction explains this distinction.

Separate the two Idgham groups

The letters ي ن م و belong to the ghunnah group; ل ر are the group without ghunnah. Apply this introductory chart with a teacher, including same-word exceptions. Do not invent an extra vowel between words to make a pattern easier to say. See the merging lesson.

Practise recognition before mixing every rule

  1. Select one teacher-assigned example.
  2. Point to the noon or tanween and the following letter.
  3. Name the rule before listening.
  4. Listen and repeat the phrase, then read it in context.
  5. Ask whether the sound and timing, not just the name, are correct.

If the learner cannot identify the letters reliably, review that foundation rather than memorize a large rule chart. If the names are secure but the sound is inconsistent, keep the task small enough for live correction.

Common traps to discuss

Stopping between words changes the exercise. A voweled noon is not noon sakinah. Idgham has exceptions that a beginner chart does not fully explain. For Iqlab and Ikhfa, do not determine mouth position from English transliteration alone. The linked Iqlab and Ikhfa lessons provide more detail.

Frequently asked questions

Can I master these rules from a table?

A table supports recognition. Accurate application also needs a recitation model and correction.

Does the same rule chart explain stopping?

No. These introductory examples concern connected reading. Learn stopping separately with the teacher.
